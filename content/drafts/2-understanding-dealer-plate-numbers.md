# Understanding Dealer Plate Numbers: What They Mean for Your Business and How to Obtain One

If you're in the car sales business or you run a fleet that regularly moves unregistered vehicles around — from the port to a showroom, say — you've probably come across "dealer plates." But here's the thing: a lot of people misunderstand what these plates are actually for, and that misunderstanding is currently getting vehicles impounded in Lagos. Let's clear it up properly.

## So what exactly is a dealer plate number?

Think of a dealer plate as a temporary, business-linked plate number rather than a personal registration tied to one specific car. Vehicle dealers are allocated their own set of plate numbers to use on vehicles that are in transit or waiting to be sold. Instead of registering every single car that passes through your lot under its own individual plate, you use your dealer plates to legally move and test-drive vehicles that haven't been registered yet.

You'll usually spot them by their distinct design — they stand out from regular plates so enforcement officers can immediately tell a vehicle is operating under dealer status, not full private or commercial registration.

## What dealer plates are actually meant for (and this matters)

This is where a lot of businesses get it wrong, and it's costing people right now. Lagos State's Transport Sector Reform Law of 2018 permits dealer number plates only for specific, limited activities — not everyday driving. The approved uses are things like:

- Authorised test drives
- Vehicle inspections
- Delivering or collecting a vehicle
- Transporting an unregistered vehicle from the port or a dealership to a specific destination

What they are **not** for is daily commuting, continuous use on public roads, or driving around a vehicle that's already been sold to a customer. That last one especially — once a car is sold, it needs its own registration, full stop.

## Why this matters right now

Lagos State has just re-issued a firm warning to motorists and car dealers about this exact misuse. The state's Vehicle Inspection Service (VIS) has already impounded 175 vehicles for violating dealer plate rules, and offenders are facing confiscation, impoundment, and possible prosecution. If your business — or your logistics team — has been quietly using dealer plates as a workaround for full registration, now's the time to tighten that up before your vehicles end up on that list.

## How to actually get a dealer plate number

If your business genuinely needs dealer plates (car dealership, auto importer, vehicle logistics company), here's the general path:

1. **Register your business properly.** You'll need your CAC certificate showing your registered company name and address — this is non-negotiable, individuals can't apply as though they're a business.
2. **Apply through the appropriate state licensing office or an accredited agent.** In Lagos, this typically runs through the vehicle licensing authorities alongside your business registration documents.
3. **Provide the required documents**, which generally include your CAC certificate, valid ID, proof of business address, and sometimes evidence of the nature of your trade (import permits, dealership agreements, etc.).
4. **Expect a processing window.** This isn't usually instant — give it a few weeks rather than a few days, and budget for the official fees plus any documentation costs.
5. **Get a defined number of plate pairs**, not unlimited use. Dealer plates are usually issued as a limited set (for example, three pairs) meant to rotate across vehicles currently in your inventory, not as permanent plates for every car you own.

## A few practical tips for staying compliant

- **Keep a log.** Note which vehicle each dealer plate is currently on, and why (test drive, delivery, inspection). If VIS ever stops you, being able to explain the specific, approved purpose on the spot makes a huge difference.
- **Don't let staff use dealer-plated vehicles for personal errands.** It's tempting, but it's exactly the kind of misuse the state is currently clamping down on.
- **Move fast on new registrations.** The moment a vehicle is sold or ready for permanent use, get it onto its own proper plate — don't let it linger on a dealer plate "for convenience."
- **Train your drivers and sales staff** on what dealer plates are and aren't for. Most violations happen because staff simply don't know the rules, not because anyone's trying to cheat the system.

## The bottom line

Dealer plates are a genuinely useful tool for businesses that move unregistered vehicles as part of their day-to-day work — but they come with real limits, and Lagos is actively enforcing those limits right now. Get your business properly registered, apply through the right channel, and use the plates strictly for what they're designed for. It's a lot cheaper than an impounded vehicle and a court date.
