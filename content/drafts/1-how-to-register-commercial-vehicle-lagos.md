# How to Register a New Commercial Vehicle in Lagos: A Step-by-Step Guide for Fleet Owners

So you just added another bus, van, or truck to your fleet — congrats, that's growth. But before that vehicle can legally hit Lagos roads carrying goods or passengers, you've got a bit of paperwork to sort out. And trust me, it's way less painful when you know exactly what's coming.

Lagos treats commercial vehicles differently from your everyday private car. There's an extra layer of registration, permits, and inspections because you're moving people or cargo for money, and the state wants to know your vehicle (and your drivers) can be trusted on the road. Here's the whole process, broken down so it actually makes sense.

## Step 1: Get your proof of ownership sorted first

Before anything else, make sure you have clean documentation for the vehicle itself:

- Custom duty certificate (for imported/tokunbo vehicles) or purchase receipt/invoice (for brand-new vehicles bought from a dealership)
- Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) and engine number clearly visible and matching your paperwork
- A valid means of ID for the company or individual registering the vehicle — for businesses, this usually means your CAC certificate too

Skipping this step is one of the biggest reasons registrations get delayed, so double-check everything matches before you move forward.

## Step 2: Register the plate number with FRSC

You'll typically need proof of vehicle ownership, a valid means of identification, proof of address, an insurance certificate, and — because it's a commercial vehicle — a roadworthiness certificate and your Tax Identification Number (TIN). Head to your nearest FRSC office or state licensing office, fill out the vehicle registration form, and make sure every detail matches your supporting documents. Mismatched details are a classic cause of rejected applications, so triple-check spellings, chassis numbers, and dates.

Once your plate is issued, verify it's genuine through the Nigeria Vehicle Verification Service (NVVS) portal — always worth doing for your own records, and handy if a driver or buyer ever needs proof down the line.

## Step 3: Get your Roadworthiness Certificate from VIS

This is where commercial vehicles get extra scrutiny. The Lagos State Vehicle Inspection Service (VIS) — formerly the Vehicle Inspection Unit — is the body that issues the roadworthiness certificate, and LASTMA has the power to impound any vehicle caught without one. For a new commercial vehicle, book an inspection slot, take the vehicle in, and be ready for the possibility of a queue — VIS centres can get busy, especially toward month-end when a lot of businesses are trying to beat renewal deadlines.

**Pro tip:** Don't wait until the exact week you need it. Book your slot as soon as you take delivery of the vehicle, not the week you plan to put it on the road.

## Step 4: Sort out your Hackney Permit

If the vehicle will carry passengers commercially (think staff buses, ride-hailing, or shuttle services), you'll need a Hackney Permit on top of everything else. This is issued through the Lagos State Ministry of Transportation and is separate from your regular vehicle licence — it's basically Lagos saying "yes, this specific vehicle is cleared to carry paying passengers."

## Step 5: Make sure every driver has a LASDRI card

Here's one people forget: it's not just the vehicle that needs registering — the driver does too. Commercial vehicle drivers, pick-up owners, and company vehicle drivers in Lagos need to be certified by the Lagos State Drivers' Institute (LASDRI), separate from their regular driver's licence — and this rule applies only in Lagos, not the rest of the country. Send your drivers through LASDRI training and certification before they get behind the wheel of your fleet vehicle — it saves you (and them) from unnecessary "wahala" with enforcement officers.

## Step 6: Get third-party or comprehensive insurance

This one's non-negotiable and applies across Nigeria, not just Lagos. Your commercial vehicle needs a valid insurance certificate before it can be registered, and honestly, for fleet vehicles carrying goods or people, comprehensive cover is worth the extra spend — one accident without it can wipe out months of profit.

## Step 7: Keep all documents in the vehicle, always

Once everything's issued, keep physical or digital copies of the vehicle licence, roadworthiness certificate, insurance certificate, hackney permit (if applicable), and the driver's LASDRI card and licence inside the vehicle at all times. Enforcement checks are common on Lagos roads, and having everything on hand keeps things smooth and quick.

## A quick recap for your checklist

1. Confirm ownership documents and VIN/engine number match
2. Register plate number with FRSC (bring ID, insurance, TIN, proof of address)
3. Get Roadworthiness Certificate from VIS
4. Apply for Hackney Permit (if carrying passengers)
5. Ensure drivers have LASDRI certification
6. Secure insurance cover
7. Keep documents in-vehicle and copies on file

Registering one vehicle is manageable on your own. But if you're scaling a fleet — five, ten, fifty vehicles — this process multiplied across every vehicle and every renewal date becomes a full-time job in itself. That's exactly why a lot of fleet owners now lean on digital tracking tools to keep tabs on expiry dates so nothing slips through the cracks (more on that in our piece on digital compliance tools for Lagos fleet operators).

Get the registration right from day one, and you'll spend a lot less time firefighting later.
